Yesterday, it just hit me that I should step back and reflect on aspiration to Safe RL PhD. Because as I dig deeper into this field, I get lost and don't know what is the purpose of learning these mathematically heavy concepts and doing experiments that actually won't give me many positive feedback. I feel like I need to sit down and calmly think about my motivation of doing this.I ask myself three questions. Why RL? Why RL-related PhD? Why safe RL? and I found my answer is actually quite clear and in chronological order. 
At the very beginning, before I came to Germany to do my Master's, I desire to pursue a PhD degree. Why? I think one of the reasons is I learn that the role of PhD student is to solve a problem that nobody has ever solved. I felt like that sounds so cool! I must find a problem and try to solve it through hard work and years and years dedication. Perhaps I should at least try to apply for it after my Master's study. Then the problem is which domain? what research area should I pursue for my PhD? At the time, I had no idea about this. I'm an engineer and I'm going to do engineering things for sure but there are tons of differennt topics I can choose within my field, specifically, in Automation, in control area. I hoped I could find out during the study in Germany. 
In second semester in FAU, I got accepted into a team project called Roboracer. That's a project about developing an autonomous racing car to navigate complex tracks. I was working on trajectory planning and controller. I don't have much to say about planning. I just used an existing optimal trajectory generation library. What's interesting is controller. My teammate and I were both working on this. As we planned, we were going to design two types of controllers as per project guidance and our discussion. One is classic and advanced control method like PID, Pure Pursuit and MPC. The other is using reinforcement learning. At the moment, I didn't know what is reinforcement learning at all. But I said I can try to learn it and implement it on our car. I also promised I would help him on MPC since it is hard to solve. Then, I started to think maybe I could pursue a PhD in control like MPC. After all, my undergraduate studies were also largely focused on control. I am familiar with feedback loop, automatic control and stuff. So I dug into MPC and I totally got bogged down into it. It is tough. I decided to quit. I was supposed to hang on a bit but I was so overwhelmed by exams and I felt like maybe it was not necessary for me to work on it. My teammate can handle it. Thus, I shift to reinforcement learning. Surprisingly, it gave me a spark of interest. I didn't do much learning. I just called an off-the-shelf RL library SB3 to train my car. You know what? Looking at it start to run from struggling to gradually getting to learn the track to roughly finishing a lap in the end really reminded me of my little cousin. When she were still a baby, I watched her learn to walk. She at the beginning just learned to crawl and then tried to stand to walk. The car's learning process just suddenly aroused my memory of this scene. It was excited and even a little bit moving, watching it to learn. It was super impressing anyway. Then naturally I thought maybe I could try this one, RL area, do a RL PhD. And of course it looks cool and promising because of fast AI development. This is the original motivation of me wanting to learn RL.
After that moment, I started to do some research about RL and its different research areas. Safe RL drew my attention since its value is to make sure RL agent can run safely in some safety critical area like autonomous driving and robotics. Makes sense! My racing car would hit the wall every once in a while. Even if I trained it the whole night, for like over 8 hours on just one track. That's a good and crucial problem to solve. In the future, autonomous car will run on the road most likely. If it still gets chance to deviate from the track, it is a danger to people. I think I might find the research area for my PhD. Also another thing about safe RL attracts me is that I always love to combine hardware and software. I don't want to just sitting there and code. I want to touch physical setups. Safe RL is obviously a topic to deploy RL on physical things. That is intriguing for me.
I've been learning RL for months. I've moved past the initial excitement of exploring the RL field. But right now, I still think it's a good thing to do and learning those concepts and implementing algorithms make me feel a sense of achievement. Even though I fail at becoming a RL PhD student, I think I would still dive into RL and contribute to RL field.
